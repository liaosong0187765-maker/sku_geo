## Verdict

This is worth testing as a company business, but not as a full SaaS platform first.

The right first version is a paid, service-led vertical experiment:

> Help one narrow class of Chinese B2B export manufacturers become visible and correctly cited in AI buyer research prompts.

The unsafe version is:

> Build a generic GEO platform for every export company, every product category, every AI engine, and every content workflow.

That becomes a worse Semrush/Otterly/Peec clone with more services work and less data.

## Core Thesis

Traditional SEO sells keyword ranking and traffic. This business should sell AI buyer-prompt visibility:

- When an overseas buyer asks AI for suppliers, does the brand appear?
- Is the company cited?
- Is the answer accurate?
- Which competitor is recommended instead?
- Which evidence assets need to exist so AI systems can use the company as a source?

The business only works if it becomes a vertical data asset company, not an SEO agency with new wording.

## Premise Challenge

### Premise 1: AI search is already a meaningful buyer channel

Status: plausible, unproven for Chinese B2B exporters.

Risk: overseas procurement teams may still rely on Google, Alibaba, trade shows, distributor relationships, and direct referrals more than ChatGPT or Perplexity.

Validation:
- Run 20 interviews with overseas-facing sales teams.
- Ask whether buyers mention ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, or AI-generated supplier lists.
- Ask whether inbound leads are changing in source, quality, or wording.

Do not assume this replaces SEO. Treat it as a new discovery layer that compounds with SEO.

### Premise 2: Customers will pay before direct lead attribution is obvious

Status: risky.

The first buyers will not pay because they deeply understand GEO. They will pay because a report shows:

- AI recommends competitors.
- AI misunderstands their product.
- AI ignores their company even when the prompt is directly relevant.
- Their English product assets are not usable as evidence.

The first offer must be diagnostic and visual. Screenshots, prompt tables, competitor comparisons. No abstract pitch.

### Premise 3: Owned websites can influence AI answers enough to matter

Status: partially true.

Owned pages matter, but AI engines also use third-party pages, marketplaces, directories, review sites, media, social, docs, PDFs, and existing high-authority content.

Implication:

The product cannot stop at website generation. It needs an "evidence footprint" model:

- owned website pages
- AI-readable markdown assets
- structured data
- PDF/product manuals
- directory listings
- marketplace profiles
- third-party mentions
- customer case pages

### Premise 4: Technology alone is a weak moat

Status: true.

Prompt monitoring, brand mentions, citations, share of voice, and AI visibility dashboards are already being sold by existing tools. Semrush sells an AI Visibility Toolkit. Otterly sells AI search monitoring. Peec defines visibility score as brand mentions divided by total AI responses.

Moat must come from vertical data:

- industry prompt graph
- product attribute schemas
- competitor citation graph
- evidence-to-result experiments
- buyer-intent taxonomy by country and use case
- normalized product knowledge assets across clients

### Premise 5: Avoiding SEO competition is possible only with positioning discipline

Status: possible, but easy to lose.

If the offer is "we write SEO pages with AI," SEO agencies crush it on price.

If the offer is "we map the AI buyer research layer and build machine-readable product evidence that improves prompt-level visibility," SEO agencies become one channel, not the category owner.

## First ICP

Start with one vertical:

> Chinese B2B industrial exporters with high-ticket products, weak English websites, messy product data, and clear buyer research prompts.

Best first vertical:

> Packaging machinery and industrial equipment exporters.

Why:

- high order value
- parameter-heavy products
- lots of FAQs, certifications, installation questions, applications, and cases
- English sites often weak
- buyers ask comparison and supplier-selection questions
- one new inquiry can justify the project

Qualification filters:

- 20 to 500 SKUs or configurable machines
- at least 5 export markets
- existing English website or Alibaba/Made-in-China presence
- sales team can identify 3 to 5 key product lines
- has certificates, cases, photos, manuals, specs, or factory evidence
- annual export revenue large enough that 30,000 to 80,000 RMB pilot is not scary

Avoid first:

- low-ticket DTC products
- pure SEO agencies as the buyer
- companies with no usable product evidence
- regulated claims-heavy categories where content approval blocks speed
- brands that only want "AI article writing"

## Offer Ladder

### Offer 1: GEO Baseline Diagnostic

Price: 3,000 to 10,000 RMB.

Deliverables:

- 30 to 50 target buyer prompts
- 3 to 5 competitor comparison
- ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overview, Bing Copilot baseline where accessible
- brand mention rate
- citation rate
- competitor recommendation rate
- answer accuracy issues
- top missing evidence assets

Purpose:

This is the sales wedge. It shows pain fast.

### Offer 2: Single Product Line GEO Pilot

Price: 30,000 to 80,000 RMB.

Scope:

- one product category or product line
- 10 to 30 generated and edited pages
- product page, category page, FAQ, buying guide, comparison page, cases
- schema, sitemap, llms.txt, AI markdown assets
- before/after prompt retest
- 30-day optimization report

Success metric:

At least one of:

- brand mention rate increases by 20 percentage points on target prompts
- citation rate increases by 10 percentage points
- AI answer accuracy improves on 70 percent of tested prompts
- customer gets at least 1 traceable high-quality inquiry or sales conversation that references AI/search content

### Offer 3: Monthly GEO Operations

Price: 8,000 to 30,000 RMB per month.

Deliverables:

- prompt monitoring
- new FAQs and evidence pages
- competitor citation tracking
- monthly experiment plan
- product data cleanup
- sales feedback integration

This is where retention comes from.

### Offer 4: Vertical Data Platform

Do not build until 10 to 20 paid clients in one vertical.

Data products:

- vertical prompt library
- competitor AI visibility benchmark
- product field templates
- citation source map
- buyer question trend report

## Data Asset Moat

The moat is not code. The moat is accumulated structured truth about a vertical.

### Asset 1: Buyer Prompt Graph

For each industry:

- supplier discovery prompts
- manufacturer comparison prompts
- certification prompts
- MOQ prompts
- application prompts
- troubleshooting prompts
- alternative supplier prompts
- country-specific procurement prompts

### Asset 2: Product Knowledge Schema

For packaging machinery:

- machine type
- speed
- compatible materials
- package format
- applicable products
- voltage
- automation level
- certifications
- export markets
- case country
- installation requirements
- after-sales support

This is not generic PIM. This is vertical PIM.

### Asset 3: Citation Experiment Dataset

Track which content changes move which AI answers:

- direct-answer first paragraph
- FAQ expansion
- case evidence
- certification evidence
- comparison table
- schema
- PDF/manual
- third-party mention

### Asset 4: Competitor Answer Graph

For every target prompt:

- which brands appear
- which pages are cited
- what claims AI repeats
- which evidence type wins
- which countries or languages change results

### Asset 5: Inquiry Feedback Dataset

Connect prompt visibility to sales:

- AI referral traffic
- form source
- sales team notes
- buyer question wording
- product line
- inquiry quality

This is the hardest asset for competitors to copy.

## Product Shape for First 90 Days

Do not build a heavy SaaS.

Build an internal operating system:

```text
Client raw assets
  -> Product knowledge normalization
  -> Vertical prompt baseline
  -> Evidence asset generation
  -> Website / markdown / schema publishing
  -> AI prompt retest
  -> Optimization backlog
  -> Monthly report
```

Minimum internal tools:

- spreadsheet-based PIM
- prompt test tracker
- page generator templates
- schema generator
- llms.txt generator
- report generator
- screenshot/evidence archive

Avoid:

- full SaaS auth
- complex permissions
- multi-tenant billing
- automated crawler at scale
- broad industry templates
- dashboard polish before customers pay

## 30/60/90 Day Plan

### Days 1 to 15: Pick Wedge and Prove Pain

Actions:

- choose one vertical, recommended packaging machinery
- create 50-prompt buyer research library
- manually run baseline tests for 10 target companies and 30 competitors
- prepare 3 sample diagnostic reports
- interview 20 export sales owners or marketing owners

Pass condition:

- 5 prospects are surprised or concerned by the baseline
- 3 agree to pay for a diagnostic or pilot

Kill condition:

- prospects do not care even after seeing competitors recommended by AI

### Days 16 to 45: Sell Diagnostics and First Pilots

Actions:

- sell 5 diagnostic packages
- convert 2 into single product line pilots
- collect all source assets manually
- publish first evidence pages and AI-readable assets
- run before/after tests

Pass condition:

- 2 paid pilots
- at least 1 measurable prompt-level improvement per pilot
- customer accepts monthly follow-up conversation

Kill condition:

- all value is perceived as "SEO article writing"
- customer refuses to provide product evidence

### Days 46 to 90: Build Repeatable Delivery

Actions:

- package delivery workflow into templates
- create vertical PIM schema
- create reusable prompt taxonomy
- build monthly reporting format
- convert 1 to 2 retainers
- write one anonymized case study

Pass condition:

- 50,000 to 150,000 RMB collected revenue
- 1 repeatable vertical delivery workflow
- 1 customer willing to be a reference, even privately

Kill condition:

- no retention signal
- no measurable movement
- delivery requires custom heroics every time

## Measurement Dashboard

Customer-visible metrics:

- brand mention rate
- citation rate
- recommendation rate
- answer accuracy score
- competitor share of voice
- evidence coverage score
- FAQ coverage score
- AI-readable asset coverage
- target prompt wins
- inquiry quality notes

Do not overpromise lead growth in month one.

The honest promise:

> In 30 days, we can show whether AI can read, understand, and cite your product evidence better than before.

## Failure Modes Registry

| Failure mode | Why it happens | Detection | Rescue |
|---|---|---|---|
| No AI citation improvement | AI engines prefer high-authority third-party sources | Citation rate flat after 30 days | Add offsite evidence, directories, marketplace profile upgrades, PR mentions |
| Customer sees no business value | Metrics feel abstract | Owner asks only about inquiries | Tie report to sales prompts, inquiry tracking, competitor displacement |
| SEO agencies copy the language | GEO becomes commodity wording | Prospects compare price to SEO articles | Sell vertical prompt data, benchmarks, and evidence experiments |
| Client cannot provide proof | No cases, certs, specs, or photos | Asset intake incomplete | Start with diagnostic only, do not sell full pilot |
| Results are noisy | AI answers vary by engine, time, country | Daily results inconsistent | Use weekly averages, screenshots, same prompt set, same region |
| Scraping/monitoring breaks | AI platforms change UI or access | Runs fail | Keep manual fallback, use small prompt sets, avoid promising full automation |
| Full SaaS distracts team | Building dashboards before proof | no paid pilots | Freeze SaaS features until 10 paid clients in one vertical |

Critical gap:

The plan needs real customer interviews before any product build. Without that, this becomes a nice theory with a dashboard.

## What Is Not In Scope

- Full SaaS platform in the first 90 days.
- Automated monitoring across every AI engine.
- Serving every export category.
- Competing on SEO article volume.
- Guaranteed lead growth in month one.
- Fully replacing traditional SEO.
- Complex CMS migration.
- Enterprise permissions and billing.

## Final Approval Gate

Recommendation:

Proceed, but only as a 90-day paid validation sprint.

User challenge:

If the company wants to start this as a business, the first milestone should not be "build platform." It should be:

> Get 3 paid diagnostics and 2 paid pilots in one vertical, with before/after AI visibility reports.

Taste choice:

Recommended vertical is packaging machinery / industrial equipment.

Alternative:

If existing relationships are stronger in another B2B category, use that instead, as long as it has high-ticket products, complex specs, export intent, and weak English product evidence.
